# PumpWatch: early warning for failing water pumps

**Problem.** Finnish municipal water utilities run thousands of pumping
stations, and most pumps are repaired only after they fail. The three
utilities we interviewed put the cost of an unplanned pump failure at
€8,000–25,000 in emergency work, not counting water outages.

**Solution.** Software that reads the motor current, pressure and vibration
data that utilities' control systems already log, and flags pumps whose
readings drift from their normal pattern. Stations that already log motor
current need no new hardware. In a six-month pilot with one utility (140
pumps), the software flagged 9 of the 11 pumps that later failed, on average
19 days ahead, and raised 14 false alarms.

**Customers.** Municipal water utilities in Finland (about 300), starting with
the 40 largest, then Sweden and Norway.

**Business model.** €40 per pump per year, so a utility with 500 pumps pays
€20,000 a year. Setup costs the utility €5,000 and takes about two weeks of
our time.

**Market and competition.** Large automation vendors sell condition
monitoring for pumps, but it needs new sensors and costs several times more
per pump. Some utilities set simple alarm thresholds themselves. We compete by
using data they already have.

**Risks.** Utilities buy through public tenders, which take 6–12 months. The
data stays on a server the utility controls. Pump data contains no personal
data.

**Plan.** Two more paid pilots in 2027 and 10 paying utilities by the end of
2028. We need €400,000 to get there.
