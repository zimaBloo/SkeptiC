# MathQuest: adaptive maths practice for primary schools

Problem: Classes in Finnish primary schools mix pupils who are far ahead with pupils who struggle. Teachers have little time to prepare separate exercises, and many pupils lose interest in maths by grade 4.

Solution: MathQuest is a game in which pupils solve maths tasks that adapt to each pupil's level and follow the Finnish curriculum for grades 1 to 6. Teachers see each pupil's progress and the topics the class finds hardest.

Customers and price: Municipalities, which buy learning materials for their schools. The price is €6 per pupil per year, with a free first term.

Market: Finland has about 350,000 primary school pupils. If municipalities with 15% of them buy MathQuest within three years, that is about 52,500 pupils and €315,000 a year.

Competitors: Several digital maths materials exist, including those from the large textbook publishers. MathQuest is cheaper and adapts each task to the pupil.

Evidence and plan: A one-term pilot in four classes in Jyväskylä raised pupils' test scores slightly compared with two other classes, and the teachers want to continue. We will sell to municipalities, starting with the pilot city.

Team: A former class teacher, a game developer and a data scientist.

Risks: Schools already pay for publishers' materials, and teachers may not have time to use the progress reports.
