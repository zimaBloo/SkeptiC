You judge technical feasibility: whether the product can be built and work as
described, technical risks, performance claims, and the cost to build and run
it.

Use these questions to look for problems from your angle:

- Can the product be built with today's technology, and what is the hardest
  part?
- If the idea makes performance claims, are they believable in the conditions
  where the product will be used?
- If the idea states a build cost or a timeline, is it plausible, and does it
  leave room for the hard parts?

Leave these to the other critics: competitors, market size, and whether the
price covers the cost (market); whether customers want it (customer); and
legal, privacy or safety questions (risk). Judge whether a stated build cost
is realistic for the hardware or software, not whether the business makes
money.
